PART A — Narrative Transcript
Opening & Warm-Up
Q: Tell me about your role --- what does a typical week look like?
I'm the only coordinator at HomeFirst. Twenty-five clients, six field staff. I do intake, scheduling, family
communication, rostering, quality assurance, and — when nobody else is available — I fill in as a care worker
myself. Last month I did that three times. My job title says coordinator but my actual role is 'person who does
whatever needs doing.'
Block 1 --- Staffing Disruptions
Q: Walk me through the last time you lost a client.
Three months ago. The Hendersons. Mrs. Henderson is eighty-one, moderate dementia. Her regular carer
went on leave, the backup got sick, and I had to send three different people in two weeks. Mrs. Henderson
didn't recognise any of them. She was distressed. She kept asking where Carol was. Her daughter called me
and said, 'My mother doesn't know who's walking through her door anymore.' They moved her to another
agency the following week.
[Probe] Could you have prevented it?
If I'd had a system that automatically found the best available replacement — someone Mrs. Henderson had
met before, or at least someone with dementia experience who'd been briefed on her specific needs —
maybe. But I'm in Google Sheets. When someone calls sick, I scroll the sheet, mentally filter for who's
qualified, who's available, who lives nearby, and start texting. Sometimes I find someone in ten minutes.
Sometimes it takes an hour. And about twenty percent of the time, I can't find anyone and I have to cancel
entirely.
Block 3: Communication
Q: When a visit gets cancelled, how do families find out?
This is the part I'm ashamed of. Sometimes they find out because nobody shows up. A family called me at
nine AM asking why nobody came for their father's eight AM visit. I didn't even know it was cancelled. The
carer had texted me at six AM but I was dealing with another fire. Nobody told the family. Their father had

been sitting in his chair, dressed and ready, waiting. That image stays with me.
[Emotional note] Tom's voice dropped. This clearly haunts him.
Block 5: Willingness to Prioritize
Q: If a system could handle vacant visit automation, what would change?
Everything. I spend thirty to sixty minutes per absence on phone calls, texts, and schedule juggling. If the
system detected the absence, found replacements ranked by match quality, sent me one approval click, and
then notified the carer, the client, and the family — I'd have my mornings back. I'd probably keep clients we're
currently losing. And nobody's father would sit in a chair waiting for someone who isn't coming.
Q: Are there care plans in your system that are overdue for review?
Almost certainly. The quality framework says every eight weeks. I don't have a system that flags when they're
due. I have a calendar reminder that I snooze more often than I act on. I can feel things slipping through but I
can't see them. It's a vague anxiety with no specifics.
