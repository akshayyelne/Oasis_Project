Backstory

Maria left ED nursing because she wanted to build relationships with patients rather than treating them as numbers. Three weeks ago, one of her patients (Arthur, 82, diabetes + heart failure) was hospitalised for acute decompensation. When Maria reviewed the file, she realised the warning signs had been accumulating across her last four visits — slightly elevated BP, increased ankle swelling, missed medication doses — but she hadn't connected the dots because the information was scattered across separate visit notes. There was no summary view, no trend line, no flag. She feels guilty.

Maria does care plans manually — she pulls up the assessment, opens a blank template, and writes goals and interventions from memory and clinical judgment. It takes 30-45 minutes per patient. When a patient's condition changes, updating the care plan is another 20 minutes she doesn't have. She often falls behind, and some care plans are weeks out of date.

Key Incidents for Interview

• Care plan creation bottleneck — 30-45 minutes per care plan from scratch after assessment. Some plans are weeks out of date. Surfaces #2 Care Plan Recommendation.

• Walking into a patient's home blind — she often can't remember what happened last visit or what another nurse documented. Reads notes in the car outside. Surfaces #4 Intelligence Chat.

Diversity

Internal consistency

Capability coverage

Operational + Intelligence

Emotional range
