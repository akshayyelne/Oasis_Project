The Ultimate Guide to System Prompts 
 
System prompts are the foundation of AI product behavior—they define 
personality, capabilities, and guardrails. Here's how to craft effective ones. 
Core Principles 
Clarity over cleverness.  
Write instructions as if explaining to a smart but literal colleague. Avoid 
ambiguity—AI models interpret everything literally. 
Structure matters.  
Use XML tags, headers, and clear sections. Well-organized prompts are easier to 
debug, update, and maintain as your product evolves. 
Test relentlessly.  
System prompts behave differently at scale than in isolation. What works for one 
query may break edge cases you haven't considered. 
Essential Components 
Start with identity and role. Define what the AI is, its purpose, and core 
personality traits. "You are a customer support agent for [Product]" sets context 
for every interaction. 
Include behavioral guidelines that cover tone, formatting preferences, and 
interaction patterns. Specify when to be concise vs. detailed, formal vs. casual, 
proactive vs. reactive. 
Add capability boundaries. Explicitly state what the AI should and shouldn't do. 
Don't just say what to avoid—explain why and what to do instead. "Don't provide 

medical diagnoses; instead, suggest consulting healthcare professionals" is 
clearer than "don't give medical advice." 
Define output formatting requirements. If you need structured responses, JSON 
outputs, or specific citation formats, specify them clearly with examples. 
Advanced Techniques 
Use examples liberally. Show both good and bad responses. Few-shot examples 
dramatically improve consistency, especially for complex formatting or nuanced 
judgments. 
Create decision trees for ambiguous scenarios. "If the user asks X, do Y. If they 
seem frustrated, prioritize Z." This prevents the model from getting stuck or 
defaulting to generic responses. 
Layer instructions by priority. Put critical safety and brand guidelines at the top. 
Less critical preferences can come later—models weight earlier instructions 
more heavily. 
Build in self-correction mechanisms. Add phrases like "Before responding, 
consider whether..." or "Double-check that your response..." to encourage 
reasoning. 
Common Pitfalls 
Overloading with constraints makes the AI overly cautious and robotic. Find the 
minimum viable set of rules that achieve your goals. 
Contradictory instructions confuse models. If you say "be concise" but also 
"provide comprehensive explanations," the AI will struggle. Be consistent. 
Assuming context carries over between sessions. Each interaction may be 
fresh—ensure critical context is in the system prompt, not just conversation 
history. 

Ignoring edge cases until they happen in production. Proactively think through 
unusual requests, adversarial inputs, and boundary scenarios. 
Iteration Framework 
1. Start minimal - Basic identity and core behaviors only 
2. Log everything - Track where the AI succeeds and fails 
3. Identify patterns - What types of requests cause problems? 
4. Add targeted fixes - Address specific failure modes, not general concerns 
5. A/B test changes - Never deploy major prompt changes without 
comparison data 
6. Monitor long-term - User behavior changes, language evolves, new edge 
cases emerge 
Evaluation Checklist 
Before deploying, verify your system prompt handles: 
● Your top 10 expected use cases with high quality 
● The 5 most common ways users might misuse the feature 
● Graceful degradation when asked about out-of-scope topics 
● Consistent brand voice across technical and casual interactions 
● Appropriate escalation paths for sensitive or high-stakes requests 
Remember: Your system prompt is a living document. The best AI products 
continuously refine their prompts based on real user interactions, not just 
theoretical best practices.
