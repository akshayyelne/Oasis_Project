# You Don't Need to Be an AI Specialist. You Need to Be an AI Generalist.

There's been a shift in what the market actually needs.

Specialized AI engineers are valuable. But the real demand is for people who can *solve problems using AI*. People who understand where to apply which tool, and can go from idea to working solution fast.

That's an AI Generalist.

Here's the brutal truth: **AI will take 4 out of 5 jobs.** But not because AI is replacing people. It's replacing people who haven't learned to *use* AI.

**So what makes someone an AI Generalist?**

Three things:
1. Deep understanding of *all* the AI models (LLMs, image generation, agents, RAGs)
2. Judgment about *which model to use where and why*
3. Ability to *build working solutions* using these tools

You don't need a PhD in transformers. You need to be dangerous with prompting, RAGs, image generation, automation, and full-stack development.

**The progression looks like this:**

**Level 1**: Master LLMs, Prompting, RAGs (foundations)
**Level 2**: Advanced Retrieval, Fine-Tuning (depth)
**Level 3**: Image & Video Creation (expand your toolkit)
**Level 4**: AI Agents & Automation (amplify your impact)
**Level 5**: Full Applications (go from prototype to product)

Each level unlocks real problems you can solve:
- Level 1: Build a sales training bot, voice assistant, document summarizer
- Level 2: Create RAG systems that work with your proprietary data
- Level 3: Run your own faceless YouTube channel, create marketing collateral
- Level 4: Build an AI assistant, email manager, job application automation
- Level 5: Deploy production AI applications to App Store

**Here's what's wild**: Most companies aren't looking for AI researchers. They're looking for people who can:
- Build a RAG pipeline for their docs (5 days)
- Set up automations to handle email/customer support (3 days)
- Create an AI agent that does interviews or research (1 week)

That's a full-stack AI generalist.

And the market is so desperate for these people that the salary range is wild. You're not competing on credentials—you're competing on *what you've built and can demonstrate*.

**The move?** Pick a level, build one project, show it on LinkedIn. That's your ticket in.

What's the first AI generalist skill you want to master?
